# No. 7 · The Roman Dodecahedron: facts and sources

Every claim the narration or the screen makes, and where it comes from. Changes from the original script are marked **changed**.

| Claim (narration / screen) | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| About 130 have been found | Wikipedia, "Roman dodecahedron": "about 130 similar objects have been found in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Hungary, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom" | **changed** from "over 100" |
| Found in the northern provinces, "from Britain to Hungary" | same; also the Hunt Museum record ("found in the northern provinces of the Roman Empire") | not in the Roman heartland in Italy |
| 2nd–4th century AD | Wikipedia | Tongeren 4002 is dated c. AD 150–400 by its museum |
| Hollow, cast bronze; 12 pentagonal faces, a knob on each corner, a hole in each face | Hunt Museum record HCM 157; PAS records | 20 corners → 20 knobs (geometry of a dodecahedron) |
| Holes differ in size | Hunt Museum record ("12 circular holes of different diameters") | visible on Tongeren 4002 in its case; measured on the 3D model |
| Much Hadham find: Hertfordshire, recorded 22 August 2018, 82 mm, 247 g, holes 13.7–20 mm (map pin, then the record's figures beside the 3D model) | Portable Antiquities Scheme record 941889, as quoted on its Wikimedia Commons file page (finds.org.uk itself was not reachable) | **changed**: the script's "metal detectorist" is not stated in the sources read, so it is dropped |
| Size range 4–11 cm (golf ball to nearly a grapefruit) | Wikipedia: "vary in size from 4–11 cm" | a golf ball is 4.3 cm; the three models and the golf ball stand at true scale beside a 30 cm rule |
| No Roman text, painting or carving shows them | Hunt Museum record: "there is no mention of them in classical sources"; Wikipedia | the script's "deliberately erased" is dropped as unsupported; the gallery shows a real Roman painting (Met 03.14.4) and relief (Met 59.11.19) as examples of what the Romans did depict |
| Theories: candle holder, surveying instrument, die, glove-knitting tool | Wikipedia (lists these among proposed uses) | |
| "No two made the same" | the Hunt record (H 4.9), the PAS record (82 mm) and the 4–11 cm range give different sizes; hole sizes differ even within one object | a summary, not a measurement |
| Hunt Museum catalogue text (on the card) | Europeana record 325/HCM157 (metadata CC0), description field, quoted verbatim | the card is a typeset transcription and says so on screen |
| Several were found in coin hoards | Wikipedia: "Several dodecahedra were found in coin hoards" | **changed** from "frequently buried with coins" |
| The coins in the soil | The Met 08.170.121 (sestertius of Trajan, 3.4 cm), 08.170.117 (Titus, 3.4 cm), 05.47 (Trebonianus Gallus, 2.7 cm) | coins of the period, not from a dodecahedron hoard; tagged as The Met's |
| The books: Vitruvius (building), Vegetius (the army), Apicius (cooking) | Library of Congress: NA2515 .V8 1521 (41 cm); Incun. X .V37 (1475, 29 cm); Incun. 1498 .A6 (19.7 cm) | printed editions of the Roman texts; the lit lines are a heading (Vitruvius' table of chapters) and the incipits |
| Hole diameters shown on the 3D model (e.g. Ø 16.5 vs Ø 15 mm) | the model's twelve holes are spread across the Much Hadham range, 13.7–20 mm | a reconstruction: the values are the model's, within the recorded range; the screen says "3D RECONSTRUCTION · sizes as recorded" |
| Find spots on the map | Much Hadham (village centre, 51.86 N 0.07 E) for PAS 941889; Limerick (Hunt Museum) | the pin marks the parish, not the exact findspot (PAS publishes findspots generalised) |

Scale: Studio and Archive sets are in metres at true size: books, coins, the painting (195.6 × 271.8 cm) and the relief (67.7 cm) as in their records. Tongeren 4002 has no size in the record read; it stands in its case at an assumed 7.5 cm and is never labelled with a size.
