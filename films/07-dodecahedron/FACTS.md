# No. 7 · The Roman Dodecahedron: facts and sources

Every claim the narration or the screen makes, and where it comes from. Changes from the original script are marked **changed**.

| Claim (narration / screen) | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| About 130 have been found | Wikipedia, "Roman dodecahedron": "about 130 similar objects have been found in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Hungary, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom" | **changed** from "over 100" |
| Found in the northern provinces, "from Britain to Hungary" | same; also the Hunt Museum record ("found in the northern provinces of the Roman Empire") | not in the Roman heartland in Italy |
| 2nd–4th century AD | Wikipedia | Tongeren 4002 is dated c. AD 150–400 by its museum |
| Hollow, cast bronze; 12 pentagonal faces, a knob on each corner, a hole in each face | Hunt Museum record HCM 157; PAS records | 20 corners → 20 knobs (geometry of a dodecahedron) |
| Holes differ in size | Hunt Museum record ("12 circular holes of different diameters") | shown on Tongeren 4002 (visible in the photograph) |
| Much Hadham find: Hertfordshire, recorded 22 August 2018, 82 mm, 247 g, holes 13.7–20 mm (shown as a typeset record summary) | Portable Antiquities Scheme record 941889, as quoted on its Wikimedia Commons file page (finds.org.uk itself was not reachable) | **changed**: the script's "metal detectorist" is not stated in the sources read, so it is dropped |
| Size range 4–11 cm (golf ball to nearly a grapefruit) | Wikipedia: "vary in size from 4–11 cm" | a golf ball is 4.3 cm; circles on the table are drawn at true scale |
| No Roman text, painting or carving shows them | Hunt Museum record: "there is no mention of them in classical sources"; Wikipedia | the script's "deliberately erased" is dropped as unsupported |
| Theories: candle holder, surveying instrument, die, glove-knitting tool | Wikipedia (lists these among proposed uses) | |
| "No two made the same" | the Hunt record (H 4.9), the PAS record (82 mm) and the 4–11 cm range give different sizes; hole sizes differ even within one object | a summary, not a measurement |
| Hunt Museum catalogue text (on the card) | Europeana record 325/HCM157 (metadata CC0), description field, quoted verbatim | the card is a typeset transcription and says so on screen |
| Several were found in coin hoards | Wikipedia: "Several dodecahedra were found in coin hoards" | **changed** from "frequently buried with coins" |
| The coins on the table | The Met 08.170.121 (sestertius of Trajan, 3.4 cm), 08.170.117 (Titus, 3.4 cm), 05.47 (Trebonianus Gallus, 2.7 cm) | coins of the period, not from a dodecahedron hoard; tagged as The Met's |
| The books: Vitruvius (building), Vegetius (the army), Apicius (cooking) | Library of Congress: NA2515 .V8 1521 (41 cm); Incun. X .V37 (1475, 29 cm); Incun. 1498 .A6 (19.7 cm) | printed editions of the Roman texts; the lit lines are their titles/incipits |

Scale: one world unit = 0.1 mm. Books and coins lie at the sizes in their records. Tongeren 4002 has no size in the record read, so it lies at an assumed 60 mm and is never labelled with a size.
